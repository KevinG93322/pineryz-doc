# Operate a vault

Creation gives a business a named vault. Operating it is the rest of the first release. A holder still moves assets by signature. The business configures the vault, accounts for it, and connects its own system.

## What the first release adds

| Piece | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Configuration | Fees, caps, and a pause. Share accounting stays [ERC-4626](/concepts/shares), including the guard against a deposit that would mint zero shares. |
| Operator | A role that can change configuration after creation. Moving the assets stays a signature by the holder, unless a later phase decides otherwise. |
| Ledger | A record of deposits, withdrawals, and redemptions. Balances remain reads from the chain. The ledger is the product’s own account of those movements. |
| Monitoring | A view of the factory, the vaults, and the registry, and whether they are healthy. |
| Operator API | A documented API a business system can call to create, read, and operate a vault. The console is one client of that API. |

## What the holder still does

Deposit, withdraw, and redeem follow [Shares](/concepts/shares). The wallet signs each one and pays the gas. A deposit approves the vault first when the allowance is short of the amount. A holder cannot take out more than the shares they hold, and cannot take out more of the asset than the vault holds.

Those movements stay on the chain. The ledger records them. The registry’s history of creations is a separate record: it lists vaults that passed the creation check, and it does not replace the ledger.

## The first release is in place when

- A business can name the asset, and strangers cannot mint it.
- An operator can set a cap or a pause without taking holder funds.
- Movements are in the ledger, and another system can call the API.

Until then, a deposit changes the vault’s asset balance, and the share accounts for it. Configuration, the operator, the ledger, and the API are the work this release builds. The work after it is on [The build](/vaultos/build).
