# The vault

A vault is an on-chain contract that accepts one underlying asset and issues shares in return. A business offers that vault under its own name. The factory chooses the vault address when the vault is created.

## One asset

Each vault holds one underlying asset, fixed when the vault is created. The factory deploys the vault for that asset. A vault built for a different asset does not match the code the factory approves, and the registry refuses to list it.

The first release keeps one asset per vault. Which asset that is, and who may mint it, is decided before the release locks. Supply of that asset is controlled. A later phase can introduce a new asset model for assets that stand for something off the chain. That phase keeps the same share standard.

## What creation does

Creation deploys a new vault contract. It does not move the asset into the vault. The vault starts empty. Value moves later, when a wallet deposits.

The factory records:

- the vault address
- the underlying asset
- the wallet that asked for the vault
- the name and symbol chosen for the shares

Those fields are also what the registry stores after the [registration check](/vaultos/registration) passes. The wallet signs the creation. The product does not take the private key.

## What a vault is for

The vault is the product a business offers without assembling the factory, the registry, and the operator tools itself. The holder sees the business’s name on the share. PineryZ supplies what runs underneath.

The first release makes that vault operable: configuration, an operator, a ledger, and a documented API. A strategy, the controls a firm needs for its own clients, and real-world assets are later work on the same vault. They are described from the [introduction](/).
