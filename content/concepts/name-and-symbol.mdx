# Name and symbol

The business chooses the vault name and the share symbol. Those two strings are what a holder sees on the share token. PineryZ stays underneath. The factory, the registry, and the means to operate the vault are the infrastructure, and the name on the share is the business’s.

## What the business sets

| Field | Limit | What the holder sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vault name | 1 to 64 bytes | The name on the vault |
| Share symbol | 1 to 16 bytes | The short ticker on the share token |

The limit is bytes, so a character outside basic ASCII can consume more than one. Empty values are rejected. Names and symbols over those limits are rejected.

The name and symbol are stored on the vault. They do not change the vault’s runtime code. Two vaults with different names can still match the factory’s approved code, because that code is tied to the asset, not to the label.

## What the name does not decide

The name does not choose the asset. Each vault still holds the one asset it was created for. The name does not assign the vault to a separate legal entity. The name does not, by itself, put the vault on another network.

White-label, at the start of the build, is the name and the symbol. The first release adds the means to operate that named vault, including a documented API. Hosted branding, and a deployment under the firm’s own brand rather than a shared service, stay open. That choice is made with the build, and it is not implied by the strings stored on the vault.
